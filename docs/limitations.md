# Known limitations

This page lists what RoutePilot does not do, what its experiments did not show, and what some choices cost. Each item says what is true, and links the result run where a run backs it. Numbers come from those runs; see [results](results.md) and [performance](performance.md) for the tables.

## Routing and prediction

- **The input-token bound is a proof only for text the gateway sends.** The bound counts every byte the gateway sends upstream plus fixed framing. A provider can add hidden prompt tokens that no byte count covers. A model can declare a `prompt_overhead_tokens` value, and its bound is then the byte bound plus that value. On the benchmark data the value is fitted from recorded train and calibration records (intern-s1: 124). Out of sample, 3 flagship test records still exceed the adjusted bound, by at most 8 tokens, and one held-out record (gpt-5, livemathbench/7) is a recording anomaly that the fit excludes. The budget overshoot counter catches anything the bound misses. Image parts are charged a fixed allowance of 2048 tokens each. That is an allowance, not a proof. Run: [inupper](../bench/results/inupper/20261028T012056Z-1212012/).
- **Without a client output cap the gateway forwards `max_tokens`, not `max_completion_tokens`.** The gateway computes an output budget when the client sets none and forwards it as `max_tokens`. OpenAI reasoning models reject `max_tokens`, so such a request to one of them is rejected upstream.
- **Lower confidence bounds are conservative.** A quality floor of 0.5 was above every model's lower bound on 59% of flagship calibration items, and the lower bounds of the live Ollama models never reached 0.4. A high floor therefore relaxes to quality on most requests: the floor cannot be shown to hold, and the router falls back to the best-effort choice. Runs: [lcb_vs_point](../bench/results/lcb_vs_point/20261028T203321Z-028d6b4/), [live_train](../bench/results/live_train/20261029T043015Z-6ecd7f2/).
- **The calibration rule fixed in advance is not the best one on this data.** The rule is isotonic regression from 1000 calibration samples and Platt scaling below that. Platt scaling has a lower pooled test ECE at every calibration size, 0.0115 against 0.0160 on the full calibration split. The shipped artifact keeps the rule as fixed. In the ablation run, an artifact with Platt scaling for every model raised test success by 2.42 points and raised mean cost by 64.2%. Runs: [calibration](../bench/results/calibration/20261028T203251Z-028d6b4/), [ablations](../bench/results/ablations/20261028T221525Z-1dadba6/).
- **The dual-mode lambda is tuned where the calibrators were fitted.** Lambda is tuned on the calibration split, and the calibrators and lower-bound tables were fitted on the same split, so the calibration success at the tuned point is optimistic. A cross-fitted variant (calibrators fitted on one half of the calibration items, lambda tuned on the other half) did not do better: its test success is 5.07 points below Best Single, against 4.55 points for the shipped point. Run: [headline, flagship pool](../bench/results/headline/20261028T203202Z-028d6b4/).
- **The out-of-distribution detector is close to inert on this data.** Its AUROC for held-out datasets against the test split is 0.557, and it flagged 2 of 3374 held-out items, so the OOD fallback almost never acts. One held-out dataset separates well (arenahard_coding, AUROC 0.919); the other two do not (livemathbench 0.437, mmlupro 0.532). In the mixed-pool artifact the OOD threshold is 1.0, so it never fires there. Runs: [shift_ood](../bench/results/shift_ood/20261028T210636Z-86f6519/), [train, mixed pool](../bench/results/train/20261028T202236Z-e49df83/).
- **At the tuned lambda the learned policy has no reward edge over one fixed model.** Its mean reward (success minus lambda times cost) is 0.4527 per item, against 0.4537 for the single model with the best reward on train and calibration (qwen3-235b-a22b-2507). The learned policy spends about five times as much per item (0.004337 against 0.0008446 USD). Run: [bandits](../bench/results/bandits/20261028T223648Z-1dadba6/).
- **Fitted prices are poor for two models.** The fit has R^2 of 0.89 for gpt-5-chat and 0.65 for qwen3-235b-a22b-thinking-2507, probably because billed reasoning tokens are not in the recorded completion tokens. Expected-cost estimates for these two are less accurate. Every reported cost is the recorded cost, not the fitted one. Records with zero recorded cost (mostly failed calls) are kept at zero. Run: [data](../bench/results/data/20261028T004933Z-bec7b21/).
- **One planned ablation was not run.** A PyTorch MLP on embeddings was left out: the project has no PyTorch dependency, and an embedding model on the request path would not meet the latency target.

## Evaluation and experiments

- **The headline targets were not met.** The targets are at least 25% cost reduction with success within 1 point of the best single model. On the flagship pool the dual operating point is 93.5% cheaper, but 4.55 points lower in success (interval -6.97 to -2.25). On the mixed pool the dual point is 23.9% cheaper at equal success, just under the cost target. Runs: [headline, flagship pool](../bench/results/headline/20261028T203202Z-028d6b4/), [headline, mixed pool](../bench/results/headline/20261028T203239Z-028d6b4/), [failure_analysis](../bench/results/failure_analysis/20261029T015809Z-00deb40/).
- **Latency of API models in offline replay is simulated.** The profiles come from public pages that mark these models deprecated. Three models show no output speed, and eight of the twelve time-to-first-token values are the median of the others. The spreads are assumed. Every table that shows these numbers says "simulated". Source notes: [bench/latency/SOURCES.md](../bench/latency/SOURCES.md).
- **Off-policy evaluation is noisy at this sample size.** With exploration epsilon 0.1 on the 1735 test items, the effective sample size is 15 to 25, the RMSE of the success estimate is 9.5 to 17.5 points, and interval coverage is below its nominal level in several rows (0.70 to 0.92 for cost). The promotion gate therefore has little power. Excluding items where a candidate returns an error can flatter a candidate that refuses hard items; the number excluded is reported. Run: [ope_validation](../bench/results/ope_validation/20261028T210706Z-86f6519/).
- **The promotion gate does not apply the literal unpaired success rule.** Its success check uses the paired per-item doubly robust difference (lower bound at least -0.005), because an identical candidate cannot pass the unpaired rule at this sample size. The unpaired verdict is still written to `gate.json`.
- **Contextual bandits did not beat the supervised policy.** Both bandits chose the lowest value of their hyperparameter grid, so a better setting may lie below the grid. The bandits see one reward per item, while the supervised policy was trained with the outcome of every model on every item. Run: [bandits](../bench/results/bandits/20261028T223648Z-1dadba6/).
- **Some test splits are small.** The mixed pool test split has 361 items and the live Ollama test split has 148, so their intervals are wide. Runs: [headline, mixed pool](../bench/results/headline/20261028T203239Z-028d6b4/), [live_train](../bench/results/live_train/20261029T043015Z-6ecd7f2/).
- **The live Ollama experiment is weak evidence.** It used three small Qwen2.5 models. On gpqa their accuracy was 4.0% to 10.6%, below what random guessing among its four options gives. The learned policy showed no quality gain over always using the largest model; it lowered cost and latency only, and the prices are assumed. Another local application shared the Ollama server during part of the first collection. Runs: [live_collect, first collection](../bench/results/live_collect/20261029T015906Z-00deb40/), [live_collect, answer-only-user](../bench/results/live_collect/20261029T042250Z-6ecd7f2/), [live_train](../bench/results/live_train/20261029T043015Z-6ecd7f2/), [live_demo](../bench/results/live_demo/20261029T043138Z-6ecd7f2/).
- **Performance numbers are single runs on one machine.** Each cell is one run, the load generator, the gateway and the mock share the CPUs, and there are no confidence intervals. The first Linux run showed a tail of about one second at 2000 requests per second without streaming. It was traced to the gateway's request log on a slow file system mount, and the primary run keeps the log on ext4. In the primary run the client-side p99 difference at 2000 requests per second with streaming is 179 ms. Runs: [overhead, primary](../bench/results/overhead/20261028T193224Z-9ac474d/), [overhead, first Linux run](../bench/results/overhead/20261028T185609Z-44f7f4d/).
- **The LiteLLM comparison uses one configuration.** It is LiteLLM 1.106.6 with 8 workers on loopback and no master key, set through LiteLLM's documented override. With no master key LiteLLM does no key lookup per request, while RoutePilot checks the tenant key on every request, so this setting can only favor LiteLLM. Other settings would give other numbers. Cells where LiteLLM saturates measure queueing, not per-request overhead. Run: [litellm](../bench/results/litellm/20261028T234116Z-ee7619a/).
- **Failover and the Anthropic adapter have narrow evidence.** The mid-stream failover arm checks the rule "no second upstream request after the first byte" on 411 cut streams. The Anthropic adapter is contract-tested against documented wire formats only and has never run against the live API. Run: [failover](../bench/results/failover/20261029T010235Z-f5ff91d/).

## Budgets and accounting

- **Worst-case reservation is safe but lowers throughput under a tight budget.** A request reserves its worst-case cost before it is served, so while several requests are in flight some are refused that their final real costs would have paid for. The concurrency test (64 clients at once, a budget for 12 answers) asserts that spend stays within the budget and that some requests are served and some refused; it does not assert how many are served. In the quota run, the budget was spent to within 0.19% (memory backend) and 0.21% (Redis backend) of the limit once the clients stopped. Run: [quota_correctness](../bench/results/quota_correctness/20261029T010202Z-f5ff91d/).
- **The Redis budget scripts are not Redis Cluster safe, and exact only for small budgets.** They compute keys inside Lua. Lua numbers are doubles, so Redis budgets are exact only below 9,000 USD, and larger `budget_usd` values are rejected when the Redis backend is used.
- **A failed budget commit is not retried.** If a commit fails (for example, Redis is down), the failure is logged as a warning and nothing counts it. `routepilot reconcile` is the check.
- **Prometheus counters of picodollars lose exactness at large totals.** They are float64 and exact only up to 2^53 picodollars, about 9,007 USD per series.
- **Usage-ledger rows can be lost.** Rows are written in batches off the request path. Rows still queued are lost if the process crashes. SQLite runs in WAL mode with synchronous=NORMAL, so the last transactions can be lost on power loss. A full queue drops rows and counts them in `rp_accounting_write_errors_total`. The current quota run lost no rows. Run: [quota_correctness](../bench/results/quota_correctness/20261029T010202Z-f5ff91d/).
- **`routepilot reconcile` has two gaps.** It can show a spurious difference for a request that straddles a month boundary. It cannot read the in-process memory ledger and exits with code 2 for the memory backend, by design.

## Reliability

- **Circuit breakers are shared by all tenants.** A breaker is per deployment, so one tenant's failures can open a deployment for everyone.
- **The bulkhead covers non-streaming requests only.** It does not cover streams.
- **A late failure of a probe stream is not recorded.** After a half-open probe stream commits, a later mid-stream failure of that stream is not recorded against the breaker.
- **A stale breaker ticket still feeds the error-rate average.** The outcome of a stale ticket is ignored by the window and by the probe, but it still feeds the EWMA error rate, which `rp_deployment_error_rate` exports.

## Operations and security

- **The gateway speaks plaintext HTTP.** The examples and the default `server.listen` (`127.0.0.1:8080`) bind loopback. Put TLS in front of the gateway for anything else.
- **`/metrics` is unauthenticated.** No series carries prompts or keys. Keep the endpoint on an internal network.
- **The admin routes have no rate limit.** `/v1/feedback` is charged against the tenant's request rate (`rpm`) like a chat request, so a tenant with no `rpm` has no limit on it either.
- **Rollbacks and canary changes can be undone by a reload.** A rollback or canary change made through the admin API, or by the guardrail, lives in memory. The next reload restores what the config file says.
- **Hot reload re-reads only the `policies` section.** Other config changes need a restart.
- **Rotated decision log files are never deleted.**
- **The compose stack is a local demo.** Grafana allows anonymous viewing, and the services use throwaway local passwords.

## Platform and measurement

- **Gateway timing metrics are only as fine as the host clock.** On the Windows development host the Go clock advanced in steps of 379,700 to 502,500 ns (about 0.4 to 0.5 ms) over five probes, and more than 99.99% of consecutive reads were equal, so sub-millisecond durations recorded there are counts of clock ticks. The performance script refuses to run on a clock step above 1000 ns unless told to. Every performance number was measured on Linux (WSL2), where the step was 30 ns in all five probes. Production targets Linux. Runs: [clock, Windows](../bench/results/clock/20261029T074230Z-e12fd9f/), [clock, Linux (WSL2)](../bench/results/clock/20261029T074240Z-e12fd9f/), [overhead, primary](../bench/results/overhead/20261028T193224Z-9ac474d/).
